## <u>**Ayurveda - an Introduction**</u>
**Ayurveda is based on the theory that everything in the universe - dead or alive - is connected.** If your mind, body, and spirit are in harmony with the universe, you have good health. When something disrupts this balance, you get sick. Among the things that can upset this balance are genetic or birth defects, injuries, climate and seasonal change, age, and your emotions. **Ayurveda (a Sanskrit word that means "science of life" or "knowledge of life")** is one of the world's oldest whole-body healing systems. It was developed more than 5,000 years ago in India.  
Ayurveda is based on the belief that health and wellness depend on a delicate balance between the mind, body, spirit, and environment. **The main goal of ayurvedic medicine is to promote good health and prevent, not fight, disease. But treatments may be geared toward specific health problems.**

## <u>**History of Ayurveda**</u> 

Ayurveda is attributed to Dhanvantari, the physician to the gods in Hindu mythology, who received it from Brahma. Its earliest concepts were set out in the portion of the Vedas known as the Atharvaveda (c. 2nd millennium BCE). The period of Vedic medicine lasted until about 800 BCE. The Vedas are rich in magical practices for the treatment of diseases and in charms for the expulsion of the demons traditionally supposed to cause diseases. The chief conditions mentioned are fever (takman), cough, consumption, diarrhea, dropsy (generalized edema), abscesses, seizures, tumours, and skin diseases (including leprosy). The herbs recommended for treatment are numerous.  
All later writings on Indian medicine were based on these works, which analyze the human body in terms of earth, water, fire, air, and ether as well as the three bodily humours (vata, pitta, and kapha).  
Those who practice ayurveda believe every person is made of five basic elements found in the universe: space, air, fire, water, and earth. These combine in the human body to form three life forces, or energies, called doshas. They control how your body works. They are vata dosha (space and air); pitta dosha (fire and water); and kapha dosha (water and earth).

## <u>**Benefits of Ayurveda**</u>
As with many alternative therapies, not as much clinical research has been done on ayurveda as on Western medicine. But many practices of ayurveda have been shown to improve health and well-being.
**Some observed effects of Ayurveda on health are :**
- Improves symptoms of rheumatoid arthritis.  
- Lowers blood pressure and cholesterol.  
- Ayurvedic diet can reduce risk of heart disease.  
- Practice of Yoga improves chronic pain, anxiety and boosts mental & physical fitness.  
- Meditation improves focus, reduces stress & anxiety and improves sleep quality.  
- Ayurvedic Practices & Practice of Yoga helps other symptoms like depression & irritable bowel syndrome.  

## <u>**Branches of Ayurveda**</u>

### Kaya Chikitsa (Internal Medicine)
The term "Kaya Chikitsa" means "treatment of diseases of the entire body". Hence, **this branch provides information on diseases that affect the body such as fever, bleeding disorders, consumption, memory issues, skin-related problems, urinary disorders, etc.** "Kaya" also means "antaragni" i.e., the fire of the physical body. So, Kaya Chikitsa suggests that the fire within the physical body should be well-maintained as it is responsible for the transformation and conversion of body cells and tissues. In case the innate fire isn't controlled then it can lead to various diseases like fever, skin issues, bleeding problems, etc.

### Bala Chikitsa (Pediatrics)
This branch of Ayurveda is also named Koumara Bhritya. **This specific branch specializes in the nutrition of children. The Diet of children, how to purify milk, and how to treat diseases that occur in children** are all mentioned in this specific division.

### Graha Chikitsa (Psychology)
Also known as "Bhuta Vidya", **this branch concentrates on diseases and problems caused by various grahas like Deva or god, Daitya or monster, Gandharva or singers from heaven, Yaksha or demigods, Rakshasa or demon, Pitru or ancestral forefathers, Pisachi or devils, etc.**

### Urdhwanga Chikitsa (Treatment of disease above the clavicle)
**This branch of Ayurveda deals with diseases and treatments of the body parts that are above the collar bone.** These body parts include the eyes, ears, nose, mouth, and neck. It is also named Shalakya Tantra. The term "urdhwanga" is made up of two words- "urdhwa" which means upper and "anga" which means organs.

### Shalya Chikitsa (Surgery)
**The shalya tantra branch of Ayurveda includes various methods through which "shalya" i.e., foreign objects are removed from the body.** These foreign objects include gravel, wood, dust, pus, contaminated wounds, etc., and can be a root cause of various diseases. It also includes the study and cure of Ama Vrana or immature wounds, Pakva Vrana or mature wounds, etc. Further, this subdivision also elaborates on Yantras or instruments, Shastra or surgical instruments, Agni Karma, etc.

### Damshtra Chikitsa (Toxicology)
This particular branch of Ayurveda is also known as Visha Chikitsa which means "treatment of poison". Hence, **it focuses on methods of treating bites and stings of various insects, rodents, and snakes which can transfer the poison to human beings.** It also elaborates on the various types of poisons, their consequences, and how to heal them. These are Swabhavika Visha or natural poison, Kritrima Visha or artificial poison, and Samyoga Visha or a combination of poisons to name a few.

### Jara Chikitsa (Rejuvenation)
Also known as Rasayana Tantra, this branch provides knowledge on how to remain young for a longer duration, how to prevent ageing, and how to elongate your life span, intelligence, and immunity. **This branch discusses how to prevent disease in the later part of life i.e., during old age and how to prevent premature ageing.**

### Vrsha Chikitsa (Aphrodisiac therapy).
The eighth branch of Ayurveda talks about methods that can be used to nourish, cleanse and improve the quantity and production of semen in those who deal with problems such as Alpa Shukra or scanty semen, Ksheena Shukra or decreased sperm count or semen, Dushta Shukra or contaminated semen, etc. **This branch mainly focuses on sexual wellness of both men and women.**

## <u>**Books on Ayurveda**</u>
**Charaka Samhita &mdash; Physician**  
The Charaka Samhita is an Ayurvedic treatise (Indian traditional medicine) written in Sanskrit. The Charaka Samhita is one of two Ayurvedic fundamental texts, the other being the Sushruta Samhita. It remained a classic text on the subject for two millennia and was translated into numerous foreign languages, including Arabic and Latin. The current version of the Charaka-Samhita is said to have developed in the first century CE.  

**Sushruta Samhita &mdash; Surgeon**  
Sushruta Samhita is attributed to Sage Sushruta. It primarily focuses on techniques of surgery. It is considered as foundational text of Ayurvedic Surgery. It provides the detailed explanation of process of surgery, plastic surgery information, cataract removal information.

**Ashtanga Hridaya**  
This book is written by Sage Vagbhata. This book synthesises the teaching from Charaka Samhita and Sushruta Samhita. This book is known for systematic presentation of Ayurvedic knowledge. It is accessible for learners and practitioners of Ayurveda.

**Bhava Prakasha**  
It is a classical ayurvedic text. It is known for writing all about Herbal medicines. It carries data about medicinal plants, properties, therapeutic use of the plants.

**Madhava Nidana**   
This book focuses on Diagnostics. It helps in identification and classification of diseases. In precise this book focuses on effective treatment planning. Thus, it concentrates on clinical practice of Ayurveda.

## <u>**Introduction to Alchemy**</u>  
Indian Knowledge Systems (IKS) also include significant contributions to the field of alchemy, known in ancient India as **Rasashastra**  
Rasashastra is the study of chemicals, minerals, and medicinal compounds, particularly focused on the transformation of materials and the preparation of elixirs and medicines.
Indian alchemy is a rich and complex field within the broader Indian Knowledge Systems, combining practical experimentation with spiritual and philosophical goals.

### Mercury and Metallurgy
Rasashastra emphasizes the use of mercury (rasa) and its compounds. Ancient texts describe complex processes to purify and amalgamate mercury with other metals like gold, silver, copper, and iron.
