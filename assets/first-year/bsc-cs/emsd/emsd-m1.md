> **What is Ecosystem? and explain its structure.**

## <u>Definition of an Ecosystem</u>

The word ecosystem means ecological system. The living organisms and non-living components are separately interrelated and also interact with each other. A functional system made up of living and non-living things is called an ecosystem.

These are living factors of the environment; hence it consists of equilibrium interacting and sustaining. In simple words, an ecosystem is a fundamental functional unit of the surface of the earth.

## <u>Characteristics of an Ecosystem</u>

- An ecosystem represents the total of living organisms and physical components in each dimension,there are interactions between the biotic elements and between abiotic factors.

- It is an open system, and this is characterized by continuous input and output of matter and energy.

- It has spatial dimensions; it may be small like a tree and large like an ocean. Thus, it shows large variations in size, structure, and composition.

## <u>Structure of an Ecosystem</u>
The structure of an ecosystem refers to the way its living (biotic) and non-living (abiotic) components are organized and interact with each other.
This structure determines how energy and nutrients flow within the ecosystem.

### <u>Biotic components (Living Things)</u>
**Biotic components** are the living part of an ecosystem and they play a vital role in maintaining the balance and proper functioning of the environment. These living organisms can be broadly classified into three main groups: **(producer, consumer, decomposer).**


- <u>**Producers**</u> are also known as autotrophs—organisms that can prepare their own food using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide through the process of photosynthesis. They form the foundation of the food chain by supplying energy to all other living beings in the ecosystem.


- <u>**Consumers**</u> are organisms that depend on other living beings for their food. They cannot produce their own food; they consume plants or other animals for their survival. Consumers are further divided based on their diet. **Primary consumers** are herbivores that feed directly on producers (for example: deer, rabbits, cow). **Secondary consumers** are carnivores that eat primary consumers (for example: snake, frog, fox, and insect-eating birds fall into this category). **Tertiary consumers** are top predators that feed on secondary consumers (for example: lion, eagle).


- <u>**Decomposers**</u> are responsible for breaking down dead plants and animals and waste material. They convert complex organic matter into simpler substances, returning valuable nutrients to the soil which producers require to grow again. This nutrient recycling is crucial for sustaining the ecosystem. Common decomposers are mushrooms, bacteria, earthworms, etc.

### <u>Abiotic components (Non Living Things)</u>
**Abiotic components** are the non-living physical and chemical elements present in an ecosystem. They play a foundational role by influencing the types of organisms that can live in a particular environment and by shaping the overall functioning of the ecosystem. Unlike biotic components, abiotic factors do not have life; however, they are essential for the survival and growth of living things.

- <u>**Sunlight**</u> is one of the most important abiotic factors. It provides the energy needed for photosynthesis, the process by which green plants and some bacteria produce food. Without sunlight, most life on the earth would not exist.

- <u>**Water**</u> is another critical abiotic component. All living organisms need water to survive, and the availability of water determines the type of plants and animals that can live in an area. Water can be found in rivers, lakes, oceans, rainfall, and even as moisture in the soil. In aquatic ecosystems, factors like water depth, dissolved oxygen, and salinity are especially important.

- <u>**Air**</u> or the atmosphere provides essential gases like oxygen for respiration and carbon dioxide for photosynthesis. The composition and quality of air affect the health of both plants and animals.

- <u>**Temperature**</u> is a key abiotic factor that affects the metabolic activities and survival of organisms. Each species has a certain range of temperature in which it can live comfortably.

- <u>**Soil**</u> is a mixture of minerals, organic matter, air, and water. It provides nutrients and a place for plants to grow. Soil also affects the animals that depend on those plants for food and shelter.

- <u>**Minerals and nutrients**</u> found in soil and water are necessary for the growth and development of living organisms. Elements like nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, and trace minerals play a vital role in plant nutrition and the food chain.
